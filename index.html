What is NERSI?

NERSI stands for National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention, a Federal Government relief programme managed by the Federal Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Reduction.

According to the official programme information, selected beneficiaries receive ₦50,000 in a mobile wallet. The support is a grant, not a loan.

Key facts

  • You cannot apply or register online for NERSI.
  • You cannot pay anyone to be added to a beneficiary list.
  • Beneficiaries are selected using verified national identity records and the National Social Register.
  • Selected people are contacted by an approved payment provider.
  • The programme says there is no fee to accept or receive support; a POS agent may charge its usual withdrawal fee.
  • Payments are made in phases, so not everyone is contacted at the same time.

How the process works

  1. If selected, you receive an SMS from ALAT, MoMo, NairaGram, OPay, or Moniepoint.
  2. The message asks for your consent, such as replying YES. Nothing is paid without consent.
  3. Your mobile wallet is linked to your phone number and NIN, as described by the official programme.
  4. After the wallet is credited, follow your provider's instructions to access the support.

Never share your PIN, OTP, password, or BVN. Verify current instructions directly with the official programme or your payment provider.

How to spot a scam

Stop and verify if a message or website:

  • Asks you to pay a fee to register, unlock, release, or process support.
  • Requests your PIN, OTP, password, BVN, or other sensitive details.
  • Asks you to click an unfamiliar link, download an app, or forward a message to claim money.
  • Claims someone can add you to the list or guarantee selection.
  • Comes from an unverified sender or asks you to provide information outside the official process.

Do not reply, pay, or enter information. Contact the programme helpline using a number verified on the official website.

What you should do

  • Check information on the official Ministry page: fmhapr.gov.ng/nersi.
  • If a message seems suspicious, do not respond or share details; call the helpline listed on the official page.
  • Never share your PIN, OTP, password, or BVN with anyone claiming to help you receive support.
  • Do not click unknown links or pay anyone to register or release funds.

Frequently asked questions

The answers below are based on the FAQ text you provided from the official NERSI website. For current instructions and answers to questions not covered here, check the official FAQ or call its helpline.

What is NERSI?

The National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention (NERSI) is a Federal Government relief programme to help households cope with living costs. The Federal Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Reduction runs it. Selected beneficiaries receive ₦50,000 into a mobile wallet.

How much will I receive?

₦50,000. The official FAQ says the full amount goes into your wallet and nothing is deducted.

Is it a loan?

No. It is described as a Federal Government grant and does not have to be repaid.

Who is eligible?

Beneficiaries are selected from the National Social Register and verified national identity records. You cannot apply, and no one can add you to the list. If selected, your provider will send you an SMS.

Why was I selected, and how did you get my number?

The official FAQ says beneficiaries are selected from the National Social Register and verified national identity records, and phone numbers come from those records. It says details are shared with the payment provider only so they can make the payment.

Is my personal information safe?

The official FAQ says beneficiary details are used to make NERSI payments and are protected under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. It also says no one from the programme will ask for your PIN, OTP, or BVN. For details about your rights and data handling, consult the official programme information and relevant privacy notices.

Who are ALAT, MoMo PSB, NairaGram, OPay, and Moniepoint?

They are the five payment providers identified in the official FAQ. It describes ALAT as Wema Bank's digital bank, MoMo PSB as a licensed Payment Service Bank, NairaGram as a digital money transfer service, OPay as a licensed mobile money operator, and Moniepoint as a licensed microfinance bank.

Is it only for some states or groups?

The official FAQ says beneficiaries are spread across all six geopolitical zones and selected on need, regardless of religion, ethnic group, or political affiliation. Payments are made in phases, so people may be contacted at different times.

Does it cost anything?

The official FAQ says accepting the payment, receiving it, and replying to programme SMS are free. It notes that a POS agent may charge its usual withdrawal fee; ask about any fee before withdrawing.
